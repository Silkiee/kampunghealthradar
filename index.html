Which vaccine, and when

Flu viruses change, so each vaccine is made for one season. Singapore uses both the Southern Hemisphere vaccine and the Northern Hemisphere vaccine, because flu here has two seasons a year.

    A personal experimental project with illustrative data

    Kampung Health Radar is a personal experimental project that shows how Singapore's weekly infectious disease figures could be turned into a map, charts and plain-language advice. All the disease figures on this site are illustrative: made up to look realistic, not taken from any official source. It is not run by, linked to or endorsed by the Communicable Diseases Agency (CDA), the Ministry of Health (MOH), the National Environment Agency (NEA) or any other government agency.

    Last reviewed 25 October 2026 · Illustrative data up to: see the top of the map

    For real figures and official advice Go to the Communicable Diseases Agency at cda.gov.sg. The figures on this site are made up and will not match CDA's.
    If you feel unwell See a doctor or visit a polyclinic. In an emergency, call 995. Nothing on this site can tell you whether you are sick.

    Why this exists

    CDA publishes a Weekly Infectious Disease Bulletin full of tables and charts written for specialists. As a member of the public, I found those documents hard to read. This site is a concept for making that kind of information easier to understand: what is going around, whether it is getting better or worse, and what you can do. It uses illustrative figures so the idea can be shown without reproducing official data.

    2. Not official, not medical advice

    • Illustrative figures, not real data. The weekly disease figures on this site are made up. They follow realistic seasons (dengue in mid-year, coughs, colds and flu around the year end, HFMD during school terms) but do not match any real week or year. Do not use them to judge what is going around. The real figures are the ones CDA publishes.
    • Figures can change. The illustrative figures may be changed or regenerated at any time.
    • Not medical advice. The advice on this site, including the flu jab guide, is general public health guidance in plain language. It is not advice for you as an individual. Ask a doctor about your own health.
    • No official announcements. Nothing on this site is an outbreak alert, a government announcement or an instruction. For those, follow CDA, MOH and NEA directly.
    • Please read it calmly. Many numbers here are estimates (see section 3). A dark colour on the map or a rising line in the outlook is not a warning that you, or your area, are at special risk.

    3. What is illustrative, and what is real

    The disease figures on this site are illustrative. Some are made up directly, and the rest are calculated from those made-up figures. None of them are official figures.

    • The share of samples testing positive for flu and for COVID-19

    How Kampung Health Radar works

    Every week the Communicable Diseases Agency (CDA) publishes a Weekly Infectious Disease Bulletin: dozens of tables and charts written for specialists. This personal experimental project is a concept for answering three everyday questions from that kind of data: What is going around? Is it getting better or worse? What can I do? All the disease figures here are illustrative: made up to look realistic, not taken from the bulletin.

    What the numbers show

    • Dengue: weekly notified cases (dengue fever plus dengue haemorrhagic fever).
    • HFMD and flu & respiratory infections: the average number of people a day seen at polyclinics. Polyclinics see only part of all patients, so treat these as a trend gauge, not a total count.
    • COVID-19: an estimate. We multiply respiratory polyclinic visits by the share of respiratory samples that tested positive for COVID-19.
    • Illustrative figures: every weekly value from 2012 to October 2026 comes from a simple model. It keeps realistic seasons (dengue peaks in mid-year; coughs, colds and flu peak from December to February with a smaller mid-year rise; HFMD rises during school terms) and adds an invented history of bigger and smaller years. HFMD starts in 2023, and flu and COVID-19 positive rates in 2026.
    • "Usual" means the median of the same week in the past 5 years of this data (2021 to 2025; 2023 to 2025 for HFMD).

    How the 3-month outlook is made

    The outlook starts from the average of the last three weeks, follows the usual shape of the same months in past years (2012 to 2025 for dengue and respiratory visits, 2023 to 2025 for HFMD), and continues the recent direction, fading it out over time. The darker band is where the number should land about half the time, and the lighter band about 8 times in 10. Both were set by testing the method on every earlier week of 2026. A wide band means recent weeks have been hard to predict. Adding past years through Add data sharpens the seasonal pattern automatically.

    "What if we act now?"

    The sliders use a simplified rule. Each round of infection shrinks in proportion to how much you cut the spread, after a short delay (about 2 weeks for dengue, while existing mosquitoes and infections run their course). Some cases, such as infections caught overseas, cannot be prevented this way, so we keep 15% of cases untouched. For flu, the action applies only to the share of respiratory illness caused by flu. These are illustrations for planning conversations, not predictions of any specific programme.

    Why the map is illustrative

    Weekly bulletins report national totals only. To show what a neighbourhood view could look like, we spread each national number across the 55 URA planning areas by approximate resident population and age profile. HFMD leans towards areas with many young children, and flu and COVID-19 lean towards older estates. For dengue we add a mild weighting for eastern and north-eastern estates and landed-housing areas, where dengue clusters have historically been more common. On top of that, each area gets a made-up local factor that changes from week to week, so small clusters rise and fade the way real ones might. The factors are set so the latest week shows a realistic spread, with most areas Very low or Low and one Very high, and the areas always add up to the national figure. Areas where few or no people live, such as Downtown Core, Tuas and the water catchments, are given small made-up resident numbers. Only the Western Islands and North-Eastern Islands are left out. None of this is real 2026 local data. Switch the map to national figures with the toggle.

    Colours compare each area's estimated rate per 100,000 residents with a typical week for Singapore (the 5-year median). Moderate is within 20% of typical, High is 20–60% above it, and Very high is more than 60% above. This is why a quiet year for dengue shows mostly pale colours, even at its peak.

    Flu jab guide

    The flu jab guide puts CDA's public advice on flu vaccination next to illustrative flu figures. Flu right now uses weekly flu positivity, with our own bands: Low is under 10%, Moderate is 10 to 24% and High is 25% or more. When flu season usually comes uses the illustrative polyclinic visits for coughs, colds and flu in 2012 to 2025. Am I due? and Travelling soon? follow CDA's September 2026 advice, the national adult and childhood immunisation schedules, and CDC advice for travellers. They run in your browser: nothing you choose is saved or sent. Vaccine dates, prices and CDA updates come from data/flu-updates.csv.
